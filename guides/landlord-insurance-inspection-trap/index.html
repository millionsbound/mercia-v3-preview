Will landlord insurance pay for cannabis farm damage? The inspection trap

Some landlord policies do cover damage from a tenant growing drugs, but the cover often comes with conditions. Published Financial Ombudsman Service decisions show how a claim can fail on those conditions even when the damage is obvious.

The case DRN-6210910

In Financial Ombudsman Service decision DRN-6210910, a landlord claimed for extensive cannabis farm damage. The policy included a specific condition requiring internal and external inspections at least every three months, or as permitted under the tenancy agreement.

The landlord had inspected every six months because the rent was paid on time. The Ombudsman upheld the insurer's refusal to pay: six-monthly inspections did not meet the clear three-month policy warranty.

A second decision: DRN-5319905 (RSA)

In ombudsman decision DRN-5319905 concerning Royal & Sun Alliance and a commercial property policy, the policy required inspections at least every three months and a written log kept for at least 24 months.

The Ombudsman did not uphold the complaint. While an RSA customer service offer of £100 stood, the substantive claim for damage remained declined. Your policy wording is the only wording that matters.

Common conditions across insurers (Allianz published wording)

Insurers like Allianz publish standard conditions for drug cultivation cover, including: full tenant referencing checks (proof of ID, bank details, employment confirmation), internal and external property inspections at set intervals, and keeping a written inspection log with photos.

A simple inspection log and referencing checks to keep

Keep a physical or digital folder containing: 1) Signed tenancy agreement; 2) Verified photo ID and proof of previous address; 3) Bank statements confirming rent originates from the tenant; 4) Written inspection reports dated every 3 months with room photos.

What to do if you have already found a cannabis farm

Notify your insurer immediately. Gather your inspection records, tenant referencing file, and police crime reference number. Obtain an itemised scope of work from a professional contractor before commencing permanent repairs.
